1. SCOPE AND JURISDICTION
This guideline governs electrical safety hazards, municipal distribution feeder issues, transformer failures, dangling overhead cables, and power surges affecting municipal infrastructure, public squares, and community installations.

2. CRITICAL AND HIGH-RISK ELECTRICAL HAZARDS
- Exposed live electrical wires hanging on roads, footpaths, or near trees.
- Transformer sparking, smoke emissions, or oil leakage indicating catastrophic failure.
- Electric shock hazards originating from municipal iron poles, pedestrian railings, or traffic signal posts.
- Underground cable burning, insulation meltdown, or sudden high-voltage surges damaging appliances.
- Extended unscheduled power blackouts across entire municipal wards.

3. EMERGENCY LODGING PROTOCOL
Step 1: If life-threatening conditions (e.g., exposed sparking wire) exist, maintain a 15-meter cordon and call Emergency 1912 / 1800-233-0001 immediately.
Step 2: Submit a grievance through the portal. The deterministic priority engine will detect Critical priority automatically.
Step 3: The rapid response electric squad will be dispatched with insulated equipment.

4. RESOLUTION TIMELINES (SLA)
- Exposed live electric wire / shock hazard: Immediate isolation within 1 hour; permanent fix within 4 hours.
- Distribution transformer breakdown: 6 to 12 hours.
- Voltage fluctuation / neutral wire fault: 8 to 16 hours.
- Cable replacement and overhead wire bunching: 24 to 48 hours.

5. SAFETY ADVISORY
Never approach fallen electric conductors during rain or waterlogging. Treat all loose wires as energized and live.
